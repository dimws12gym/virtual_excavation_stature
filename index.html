Expedition field guide

Careful fieldwork, stronger evidence.

  1. Excavate. Select a tool below the trench. The trowel removes the upper sediment in one action. Once a find is visible, switch to the brush: two gentle strokes recover it. A trowel used directly on a fragile exposed find can cause a simulated break when the damage option is on.
  2. Inspect and sort. The magnifier opens an enlarged specimen view, with zoom and position controls. Make your own visual assessment and choose a tray at any time. The magnifier does not display an identification or an answer hint.
  3. Reassemble. Select an adult bone card, then its matching faded outline; dragging also works. A damaged adult group must first be refitted by aligning its two virtual fragments. The individual’s right is on the viewer’s left.
  4. Assess biological sex. Use both recorded pelvic observations for the adult. Juvenile, cat and non-skeletal finds remain in separate trays.
  5. Measure. Pull the tape’s blue handle from the zero mark to the fixed femur’s farthest endpoint. Drag with a mouse or finger. The bone and tape share the same centimetre scale. When the handle is focused, arrow keys adjust the tape by 0.1 cm.
  6. Calculate and report. Use the supplied adult equation. Enter a scientific conclusion and, optionally, an imaginative scenario. Complete the keyword-based scenario challenge. Save a text report or print to PDF.

All human cases are fictional. Fracture, refitting and morphology are simplified classroom mechanics. Rejoining fragments here does not reproduce real conservation or validate measurements of damaged archaeological bones.

Use Tab to reach controls and Enter or Space to activate. No timer or lives. The mission stays in this open tab; save your report before closing or restarting.
Teacher’s method note

Evidence, equations & limitations.

These are fictional adult cases with a fictional mixed assemblage. Assemblage separation is a simplified visual classroom exercise. The magnifier enlarges schematic bones without displaying identification clues; these drawings are not sufficient for real-world species or age identification. No adult sex or stature equation is applied to the juvenile or animal material. Skeletons and pelvic outlines are simplified teaching diagrams, not diagnostic anatomical models. The case’s recorded pelvic traits provide the evidence for the exercise. Biological sex estimation does not establish gender identity.

Male: H = 2.38 × F + 61.41 ± 3.27 cm
Female: H = 2.47 × F + 54.10 ± 3.72 cm

F is maximum femur length in centimetres. H is estimated living stature. The equations requested for this activity are historically labelled “White” reference equations in the Trotter–Gleser tradition. This is a historical sample category, not a biological race determination. Ancestry is not inferred in this game, and these equations are not universal.

± is the standard error of estimate (SEE). It is not a guaranteed range, a 95% interval, or a measurement error. The report provides a central estimate and SEE separately. No age correction is applied in these fictional adult exercises.

  • Predicting skeletal stature using ancient DNA — supplementary comparison table includes the exact male and female equations used here.
  • Introduction to Human Osteology: Stature Estimation — includes the female equation; its male femur equation is a different published variant (2.32F + 65.53 ± 3.94). This game deliberately uses the requested 2.38F + 61.41 ± 3.27.
  • Sex Estimation of the Pelvis — pelvic morphology and comparative assessment.
